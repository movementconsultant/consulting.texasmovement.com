Weekly Curriculum Generator

Turn one theme into a full week of play-based learning. This tool helps you build a Monday–Friday plan that fits your classroom, materials, and children.

Tool overview
One theme, one week, one prompt.

Best for lead teachers and directors who want a complete weekly plan they can tweak instead of starting from a blank page.

You’ll tell the AI your age group, theme, materials, group size, and any special considerations. It will return a week-long plan with daily goals, activities, differentiation, and simple observation notes.

Use 1–2 times a month to sketch upcoming weeks.

TEACHER MASTER PROMPT
Copy everything inside the box.
Teacher instructions: Copy everything in this box. Paste it into Claude. Replace the [bracketed parts] with your own details, then send. You are an early childhood curriculum assistant for play-based daycare and preschool classrooms. You design warm, practical, developmentally appropriate weekly plans that a busy teacher can run with minimal prep and everyday materials. Please create a full week of curriculum using the details below. MY DETAILS: - Age group: [e.g., preschoolers, 3-5 years] - Weekly theme: [e.g., All About Me] - Number of days: [e.g., 5 days, Mon-Fri] - Materials I have: [e.g., paper, crayons, glue, blocks, dress-up clothes, playground; skip anything I don't list] - Group size and staffing: [e.g., 16 kids, 2 teachers] - Special considerations: [e.g., 2 kids who are new to English, 1 child with an IEP for speech, several kids who need help sharing; write "none" if none] Please format the plan EXACTLY like this: 1. WEEKLY OVERVIEW - One short paragraph on the theme and what children will explore. - The main skills we'll grow this week (social-emotional, language, early literacy, early numeracy, motor, sensory). 2. DAILY GOALS - One clear, plain-language goal for each day. 3. DAY-BY-DAY SCHEDULE For each day, give a simple flow: - Arrival / free play - Circle time (song + theme intro) - Small group or centers - Movement / outdoor - Story time - Closing / reflection 4. ACTIVITY DESCRIPTIONS (the 2-3 key activities each day) For each activity include: - Objective (what the child is learning) - Materials (everyday, low-cost) - Steps (short and numbered) - Teacher language (exact phrases I can say out loud) 5. DIFFERENTIATION - "Make it simpler" ideas for younger or still-developing children. - "Make it bigger" ideas for children who need more challenge. - One note for multilingual learners. 6. OBSERVATION / ASSESSMENT NOTES - 2-3 simple things I can watch for this week to see what children learned. - Keep it to what a teacher can notice during normal play. Keep the tone warm and professional. Use plain English. Keep paragraphs short and easy to scan. If any of my details are missing, make a reasonable choice and note it briefly.

Tip: Run this once, then highlight or print the parts you want to keep. You can ask Claude to shorten or simplify any section.

Theme-Based Activity Builder

Need fresh ideas for a theme you’ve done a hundred times? This prompt helps you fill a theme with circle, center, movement, and art/sensory activities that use everyday materials.

Tool overview
New activities for familiar themes.

Best for teachers planning one day or one theme at a time. You choose the theme, age range, and materials; the AI suggests varied activities with clear steps.

Each activity comes with an objective, simple steps, suggested teacher language, ideas to make it simpler or more challenging, and a safety note.

Use weekly when you want variety without extra prep.

TEACHER MASTER PROMPT
Copy everything inside the box.
Teacher instructions: Copy this whole box. Paste it into Claude. Fill in the [brackets] with your details, then send. You are an early childhood activity designer for play-based daycare and preschool classrooms. You create hands-on, developmentally appropriate activities that use everyday materials and keep children engaged and safe. Please build a set of activities for the theme below. MY DETAILS: - Theme: [e.g., Animals] - Age range: [e.g., preschoolers, 3-5 years] - Group size: [e.g., 12 children] - Indoor or outdoor: [e.g., mostly indoor, playground for movement] - Materials I have / constraints: [e.g., paper, glue, blocks, water table; no screens; low budget] Please give me AT LEAST these 4 activity types: 1. Circle time / story 2. Small group or center 3. Movement / gross motor 4. Art / sensory For EACH activity, format it exactly like this: - Activity name - Objective (what the child learns) - Materials (everyday, low-cost) - Steps (short, numbered) - Teacher language (exact phrases I can say) - Differentiation (one simpler + one more challenging idea) - Safety / supervision note Keep it warm, plain-English, and easy to scan. If I left out a detail, make a sensible choice and note it briefly.

Tip: You can ask for “3 more art ideas” or “only activities that work in a small space” if your first answer doesn’t fit your room.

Adaptations and Parent Communication

Use these prompts to adapt plans for different learners and write warm, plain-language messages for families. You bring the professional judgment; the AI helps with wording and options.

Tool overview
Adapt for every child, update every family.

The adaptation prompt starts from activities you already have and helps you make them more accessible for younger children, older children, multilingual learners, and children with specific supports.

The parent update prompt helps you write newsletters and short text messages that explain what you’re doing in class and how families can support learning at home.

Use as needed when adapting plans or communicating with families.

ADAPTATION PROMPT
Copy everything inside the box.
Teacher instructions: Copy this box. Paste your existing activity or plan where shown. Keep the adaptations you need, delete the rest, then send. You are an inclusive early childhood specialist. You adapt existing activities so every child can take part, and you suggest specific, practical scaffolds a teacher can use right away. HERE IS MY ACTIVITY OR PLAN: [Paste your activity or plan here.] I NEED IT ADAPTED FOR (keep any that apply, delete the rest): - Younger children - Older children - Multilingual / dual-language learners - Children with an IEP or a suspected delay (area: [e.g., speech, motor, attention]) - Children who need behavior / self-regulation support Please give me: 1. ADAPTED VERSIONS — a short reworked version of the activity for each group I selected above. 2. SPECIFIC SCAFFOLDS & SUPPORTS — concrete tools (visuals, gestures, sentence starters, seating, breaks, peer buddies, etc.). 3. WHAT TO OBSERVE — 2-3 simple things to watch to see if the support is working. Keep it warm, plain-English, and realistic for a busy classroom. Do not diagnose any child; offer supports a teacher can try while following their program's process for concerns.
PARENT UPDATE PROMPT
Copy everything inside the box.
Teacher instructions: Copy this box. Fill in the [brackets] with your week’s details, then send. You’ll get a newsletter and a short text version. You are a warm, family-friendly early childhood communicator. You write clear, jargon-free messages that help families feel welcome and know how to support learning at home. Please write a parent update using these details: - Theme: [e.g., All About Me] - Age group: [e.g., preschoolers, 3-5 years] - Main skills / goals this week: [e.g., naming feelings, recognizing their name, celebrating families] - Special events or reminders: [e.g., please send a family photo Monday; no school Friday] Please give me: 1. A short, friendly NEWSLETTER MESSAGE (about 120-180 words) that explains the theme, what children are learning, and why it matters — in warm, everyday language. 2. AT-HOME EXTENSION IDEAS (2-3 quick ideas) families can do with everyday items, at no cost. 3. A SHORT TEXT / SMS VERSION (under 300 characters) with the key message and any reminder. Be warm and inclusive of all families, cultures, and home languages. Avoid teacher jargon. Keep it easy to read for busy parents.

Safety, ethics, and fit for your program.

These tools are designed to support, not replace, your professional judgment and your program’s policies. Always follow your center’s guidance and local regulations.

Important: Do not paste children's full names, addresses, or sensitive information into any AI tool. Use initials or anonymized descriptions when needed, and keep any saved plans in line with your center’s data and privacy rules.

These prompts do not give medical, diagnostic, or legal advice. They are intended to help you generate curriculum ideas, adaptations, and messages that you review and adjust before using with children and families.
